## Done — new synth: **Spectral Field** (`spectrum`)

I implemented your idea as a new sonification variant. The core move was to take the brief literally: the photon is a **white-noise beam**, a mirror is a **subtractive color filter**, and reflection drives a **moving stereo source**.

### How it maps to your brief
- **"color is spectrum"** → RGB maps to three noise bands: **R = low** (~360 Hz low-pass), **G = mid** (~1050 Hz band-pass), **B = high** (~1500 Hz high-pass). White = full-spectrum noise.
- **"color filter — subtractive from noise"** → every event opens only the band(s) for its active channels and cuts the rest. A red-only beam is low rumble; white is broadband. True subtractive synthesis.
- **"reflection moves sound in direction, then sound source moves to it relative/absolute"** → a per-wave accumulator advances by the beam's horizontal travel direction, drifting to ±1.0 (100% stereo) when unobstructed. One `drift` slider blends **relative accumulation** ↔ **absolute board-column** tracking.
- **Your example, realized:** a white ray into a colored mirror is actually a *split* in this engine. So it spawns **two voices** from one event — the reflected color deflects (pans toward the edge, drifts), the passthrough continues straight (stays centered). I verified headlessly: white → red mirror yields `refCh=1` (low) deflecting right and `passCh=6` (mid+high) going straight; the red branch then carries `dirOut=RIGHT` on every downstream cell, so the low frequencies glide to 100% stereo. Exactly your picture (mirror orientation just flips left/right).
