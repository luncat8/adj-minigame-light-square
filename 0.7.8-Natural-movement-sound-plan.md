# 0.7.8 · Natural movement sound

## Sound choices and defaults

The movement selector offers `natural` (natural mechanism), `drum` (drum mechanism) and `bearing` (s2). The s2 label retains the existing bearing timbre and storage key. New/default Glass Harp settings use natural mechanism. Voice changes select drum for Spectrum Drift and natural for every other voice. Manual selections persist until the next voice change, including across reloads; existing saved bearing choices remain s2.

## Natural mechanism

`soundfxNatural.js` owns material-like movement foley, called through `soundfx.js` for both rotations and rail movement. A cached noise excitation feeds fixed, broad inharmonic resonances. Overlapping, irregular brush contacts replace periodic flutter; muted slot knocks and a lower landing contact replace melodic pings. No tonal oscillator, pitch sweep or periodic LFO is used.

Outer rings are lower, heavier and longer than inner rings. Quarter turns mark 6/4/2/1 slots; single steps mark one. Rail movement tracks stereo position, marks every crossed column (up to twelve), and has a separate brief contact at the end stop. Width zero centers the sound. Closely spaced moves attenuate subsequent contacts and shorten the texture. All sources stop within a bounded registered voice lifetime; the shared audio bus retains mute, volume and voice stealing ownership.

## Validation

- Syntax checks for changed JavaScript; sound smoke covers persistence, auto-selection, noise-only excitation, fixed resonances, contact counts, ring mass, rail pan, end stops and bounded source lifetimes.
- DOM and contract fixtures verify selector labels, defaults and classic script load order; full fixture suite covers boot integration.
- Manual listening: compare natural mechanism, s2 and drum on headphones; try outer/inner/center, quarter/single, CW/CCW, long rail drags and end stops. Confirm natural feels tactile rather than melodic, stays audible beneath the photon voice, and remains controlled during fast movement. Check mute, zero width, reload and voice switching in both file and HTTP modes.
