# 0.7 · Sound: photon sonification and sound panel

Sound is an optional, initially silent output of the visible light trace. A slow photon wave makes interactions audible by time, position, direction and color; a quieter bed reflects the visible receivers and aggregate progress. Sound never changes tracing, scoring or discovery. Provide two substantially different selectable voices, and evaluate them by listening before adding more.

## Transport events (trace.js)

Keep the current BFS, channel-mask deduplication and receiver/segment results unchanged. `traceLight(tiles, emitter, collectEvents = false)` enables an event log for live and preview traces; generation and other callers leave it off. Test seeds can opt in through the reset/seed API. Reset the log every trace, including traces with collection off, so stale events cannot be published.

- Depth is arc length in cells from the emitter at row 14, depth 0. The initial interior seed is depth 1; each propagation adds 1, including the border step. Store depth **per queue entry**, alongside its existing packed cell, direction and channel bits. The depth is not a property of `(cell, dir)`: different channels may reach the same key by different routes. The FIFO processes entries in nondecreasing depth; equal-depth ties follow deterministic enqueue order. The first arrival of each channel wins, as in the existing tracer. A later path whose channels are already visited makes no new event; this is a sonification of the finite deduplicated trace, not a simulation of indefinitely circulating photons.
- A processed glass cell produces one event: `glass` for a clear tile, `filter` if all incoming channels pass a colored mirror, `reflect` if all reflect, or `split` if both parts are nonempty. Include cell, depth, incoming direction and channel mask, passed mask, reflected mask and reflected direction. A border arrival produces `absorb` with border cell, arrival direction and arriving channels, even on the bottom. Empty non-glass interior cells are silent.
- Record border arrivals at propagation time, but do **not** assume log order is chronological: an absorb at depth d+1 can be recorded before a glass interaction at depth d. The score builder sorts by depth, retaining original event order for ties. Separate arrivals at the same receiver remain separate events, while the existing receiver buffer remains their union.
- Use preallocated typed arrays for events, with a count exposed through lazy getters as for `traceRecv` and `traceSeg`. With 169 interior cells, four directions and three channel bits, at most 2028 queue entries can be accepted. Each entry yields at most one glass event and two border arrivals, so capacity 6084 is a conservative bound for one reset/run with normal channel seeds. Assert the bound in fixtures; never silently wrap. The score copies the log before any subsequent trace resets it. No per-key bend or bounce state is needed for the first two variants.

## Score (sonify.js, DOM-free)

`sonifyBuild` takes the event snapshot, visible receiver masks, the permitted aggregate feedback and settings, and builds an owned score, independent of the tracer's reusable buffers. Each note includes depth/time, kind, cell, incoming and outgoing direction, channel masks, pitch, velocity and pan. The bed descriptor is also copied. Sorting and allocation are allowed at publish time, not in the animation loop.

- **Time:** depth / speed, in cells per second (1–16, default 4). An empty straight shot reaches the top at 3.5 s. Events at equal depth form a chord. A soft launch sound makes a straight flight audible before its arrival.
- **Space and motion:** column maps to stereo pan, scaled by width; a short pan ramp follows the outgoing direction. Row affects brightness. Reflected left and right have distinct intervals or timbres, and an arrival at the bottom has a falling, darker envelope.
- **Color and pitch:** R, G and B select distinct scale tones; combined channels sound a chord. Normalize each channel voice by `1 / sqrt(3)` (and apply a fixed safe overall gain), so a split partitions rather than duplicates white's nominal power. Pitch maps column to scale degree and row to octave band; use major pentatonic by default. Keep all pitches, including color offsets, in the selected scale. Receiver position and arrival color determine its note(s).
- **Information boundary:** by default, interaction and receiver notes use only the visible trace. If Current matches is enabled and the view is live, the existing `overlays.current` status can additionally distinguish match and partial for a lit receiver; status 0 remains neutral, not an inferred outside/target category. With the overlay disabled or during preview, do not inspect per-cell contract requirements or statuses. Fixtures compare scores for identical traces and aggregate readouts but different hidden contracts, including preview.
- **Bed:** lit receiver masks form a bounded, normalized chord. Live tension uses only the displayed percentage, solved state and feedback flags (`wrongColor`, `missingLight`, `forbiddenLight`); it may add gentle tremolo or dissonance. Preview readout still describes the live pose, so preview bed uses visible receiver masks only, not the witness contract result. Crossfade bed changes, including preview entry and release.
- **Density:** cap at 6 events per depth and 160 per wave. Select deterministically by priority absorb > split > reflect > filter > glass, then earlier depth and original order; apply the per-depth cap first and then the wave cap, finally restore chronological order. Do not merge different positions or directions into a misleading event. The bed uses the complete receiver buffer regardless of note caps. Record dropped counts in offline measurements, and revisit caps if normal boards lose important interactions.

## Engine (audio.js)

- Create and resume `AudioContext` only in a direct user gesture enabling sound. Sound remains off after reload even if preferences were saved. Handle rejected resume or unavailable Web Audio by leaving controls usable and showing a concise status; allow a later gesture to retry. Mute ramps master gain down immediately, cancels pending wave timers and suspends after the ramp. Hidden pages suspend; becoming visible resumes only if sound is still enabled, handling asynchronous suspend/resume races. Never auto-enable sound on visibility change.
- Use wave and bed buses, a conservative master gain and compressor before destination. An optional shared generated stereo impulse response provides reverb without network or audio files; bound its duration and regenerate only on a debounced settings change. Bound total live voices to 32, including multi-channel notes and bed voices; release stolen voices with a short ramp and disconnect ended nodes. Do not treat a compressor as an absolute clipping guarantee; leave headroom and verify levels by ear and meter.
- Schedule notes on the audio clock using a 25 ms lookahead timer and a 120 ms horizon, with a cursor over the sorted score. A wave owns its gain and source nodes. A new pose fades the old wave over 80 ms, stops/disconnects its scheduled and playing sources, and starts the latest score after a 120 ms move debounce. No timer or old scheduled source may start after cancellation, mute, hiding or preview release. Bed transitions crossfade over 400 ms.
- Modes: on move (default), loop (relaunch after the last note's tail plus a gap), and bed only. Off is controlled by the sound toggle, not a second mode. Replay starts a wave for the current pose. Preview enters immediately with a quieter low-pass ghost wave; release cancels it and restores the live bed. A transition into live `won` plays one resolution flourish; preview, hint republish and an already-won restart do not trigger it. Reset the transition baseline for each new puzzle.
- Distinguish pose changes, new puzzle, preview entry/release and overlay-only republishes explicitly at the game boundary. Republishing hints updates permitted note statuses/bed but does not restart a wave. Keep a copied current score for replay and for changes made while sound is off; never retain mutable trace, view or overlay buffers. Speed or voice changes affect the next wave, while volume and bed level update smoothly now.

## Variants (patches.js)

Both variants consume the same bounded event score and respect all play modes. A patch table supplies labels, parameter descriptors and note/bed voice factories; the panel builds variant controls from descriptors. Patch-specific sources count toward the global voice cap.

1. **Glass Harp** (default): FM bell reflections with different left/right ratios, a soft sine filter-pass hum, quiet band-passed clear-glass tick and longer receiver bells. Parameters: bell decay, brightness, tick level.
2. **Pulse:** short pitched percussion for glass, passes, turns and splits, plus a plucked receiver tone. Quantize to a subdivision derived from speed without moving an event earlier than launch; simultaneous depth events remain simultaneous. It works as a single wave in on-move mode as well as in loop mode. Parameters: swing, drum tone, bass level.

A sustained branch pad, spatial comet or granular texture are candidates for later listening experiments, not requirements of this first implementation. Continuous branch voices need explicit branch identity, split/merge and lifetime semantics that the deduplicated event stream does not provide; do not pretend a sequence of independent events supplies them. Add that representation only if listening shows it is worth its complexity.

## Sound panel and ownership

Add a `Sound` fieldset in `#panel` with accessible labels and `snd-` IDs checked against classic-script globals. It has an enable/mute button (`aria-pressed`), variant, volume, speed, mode and replay; a details section contains scale, root, stereo width, reverb, bed level and the selected patch's parameters. Key `M` toggles mute except when focus is in an editable control. Store validated settings under one localStorage key, with per-variant values; catch storage access failures (including file:// restrictions) and continue with in-memory defaults. Persist preferences, not permission to start audio.

- `trace.js`: optional event capture, queue depth and event getters.
- `sonify.js` (new): owned score, scale and mapping, caps, information boundary.
- `patches.js` (new): synth variants and parameter tables; split by feature before ~500 lines.
- `audio.js` (new): context lifecycle, mixing, scheduling, cancellation and modes.
- `soundpanel.js` (new): controls, settings and persistence.
- `game.js`: publish explicit transition reasons and send a snapshot to sound alongside the renderer; preview never mutates live progress.
- `app.js` and `index.html`: boot wiring, markup and load order `… render.js, sonify.js, patches.js, audio.js, soundpanel.js, game.js, input.js, app.js`. Update the AGENTS.md map. Classic-script bindings and node-shim prefixes must remain unique and must not collide with element IDs; read cross-file values lazily.

## Build order and validation

1. Add opt-in trace events and targeted fixtures; check existing trace outputs unchanged with capture both on and off. Benchmark generation separately with a fixed seed corpus, warmup and repeated paired runs, reporting medians and environment; compare capture-off performance before/after, investigate a repeatable regression above 5% rather than interpreting a single noisy run as decisive. `generate_fixtures.js` currently has no `gen_ms` timer, so use a dedicated measurement script.
2. Build the pure score and fixtures. Measure event counts, durations, per-depth density and dropped events over generated starts and witnesses. Choose cap adjustments from those measurements and listening.
3. Add the engine, Glass Harp, minimal panel and game sink. Shared files/load order require all five existing experiment scripts plus the new fixtures. Add Pulse and remaining settings after the first end-to-end listening pass.
4. Complete lifecycle and panel tests, then tune both variants in a browser. Record results and decisions in the worklog.

**Fixtures:**
- Trace: straight absorption at depth 14; both diagonals and turn directions; partial split; bottom return; multiple channels reaching one `(cell, dir)` at different depths; equal-depth glass and earlier-recorded border arrival; loop termination; capacity and no stale log after an uncaptured run. Verify `traceRecv` and `traceSeg` unchanged and queue-order invariants still hold.
- Score: chronological stable ordering, pitches in scale, pan range, split channel power, caps and deterministic overflow; snapshot survives a later trace; identical permitted inputs do not leak hidden receiver status, including preview; bed uses only allowed feedback.
- Integration: no context before a gesture; rejected resume/storage failure; time-ordered scheduling; drag burst; cancellation of already scheduled sources; preview release; no duplicate win flourish on republish/new puzzle; mute and hide/show races; preference round-trip without autoplay. Exercise all classic scripts together in the VM scope check.

**Manual pass:** on headphones and speakers, in Chrome, Firefox and Safari via file://, listen to both variants: straight flight, distinguishable turns and split, receiver pan, bottom return, fast dragging without clicks or clipping, two-minute loop, immediate mute, ghosted preview and one win flourish. Check keyboard focus, settings reload and browser performance. If a browser blocks file:// storage or audio, report the observed limitation rather than claiming it passed.
