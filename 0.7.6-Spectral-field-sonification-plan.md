# 0.7.6 · Spectral Field: subtractive color-noise sonification

A new synth variant that treats the photon as a **white-noise beam** and the mirror
as a **subtractive color filter**. Color becomes spectrum, geometry becomes a moving
stereo source. It is the literal reading of: *"color is spectrum · color filter subtractive
from noise · reflection moves sound in direction, then sound source moves to it relative
to previous direction | absolute."*

## Concept

- **Color is spectrum.** RGB maps to three noise bands: R = low (low-pass ~360 Hz),
  G = mid (band-pass ~1050 Hz), B = high (high-pass ~1500 Hz). White = full-spectrum
  noise; a single color = only its band survives. This *is* subtractive synthesis: start
  from a full-noise source, cut every band the light does not carry.
- **Color filter subtractive from noise.** Each event opens only the band(s) for its
  active channels. A red-reflected beam is low-passed rumble; a white beam is broadband.
- **Reflection moves the source, then it keeps moving.** Pan follows the beam's travel
  direction. A per-wave accumulator (`acc`) advances by the horizontal component of each
  event's outgoing direction; with no obstacle it drifts to ±1.0 (100% stereo) over a
  handful of steps. `drift` blends relative (accumulating) and absolute (board-column)
  tracking, so both readings of the brief are supported by one control.
- **Split is the headline case.** In this engine a colored mirror hit by white light is a
  *split* (`refCh` + `passCh` both non-zero): the reflected color deflects in the new
  direction, the passthrough continues straight. So a white ray hitting a red mirror yields
  exactly the brief's picture — low frequencies peel off toward the deflection while the
  rest stay centered — realized as two voices from one event.

## Score fields added (sonify.js)

`note.refCh`, `note.passCh` (the reflected and passed channel masks) and `note.dirIn`
(the incoming travel direction). Additive to the preallocated note struct; existing
variants ignore them and fixtures are unaffected (verified: sonify_fixtures 180/180).

## Implementation (patchesFlow.js)

- `pfSpectrumColor(ac, src, sum, ch, p, bright, when)` — connects the noise source to the
  sum through the band filter(s) for `ch` (white = single ceiling low-pass; single/pair
  colors = their bands). Brightness tilts the spectral balance (top = more air, bottom =
  more rumble).
- `pfSpectrumNote(ac, bus, n, p, when, wave)` — one moving-source voice per event:
  - white-noise `BufferSource` → `pfSpectrumColor` → env gain → `StereoPanner` → bus.
  - pan ramps from the running `sp.curPan` to the target over the inter-event gap, so the
    source glides continuously; `sp.curPan` is the shared moving position.
  - target = `(1-drift)·n.pan + drift·(useDrift ? acc : n.pan)`. On a split, the reflected
    color voice uses `dirOut` (deflected, drifts); the passthrough voice uses `dirIn`
    (straight, positional). On reflect only `refCh` sounds (deflected); glass/filter/absorb
    sound `ch` in their travel direction.
  - registers the env gain with `wave.voice` so the 32-voice cap and stealing still apply.

## Variant entry (patches.js)

`id: 'spectrum'`, `label: 'Spectral Field'`, reuses the shared `paBed`. Params (sliders,
auto-discovered by the Sound panel):
- `drift` 0–1 (def 0.75): relative accumulation ↔ absolute board position.
- `driftStep` 0.05–0.5 (def 0.16): how fast the source drifts to the edge per step.
- `reso` 0.5–6 (def 1.6): band separation sharpness (how distinct R/G/B sound).
- `bright` 0.3–1.5 (def 1.0): overall spectral brightness / noise level.

## Manual pass list

- A white ray sounds like full noise; a red-only beam sounds low and dark.
- A white ray into a colored mirror clearly splits: one color deflects (pans to the
  edge over a few steps), the other continues centered.
- `drift = 0` anchors pan to board column (absolute); `drift = 1` lets the source drift
  fully with direction (relative).
- `reso` makes the three colors more or less distinct; `bright` tilts the spectrum.
- Loop mode stays calm; mute and preview behave like the other variants.
